### 8. The nightly Jobber cron can no longer overwrite a Calendar-mastered date

The real finding behind Thursday's audit note.

`daily-jobber-anomaly-reconcile` picks its candidates by **Jobber GID**, and its own comment called
that "Jobber-sourced". It is not: **every Calendar-created visit gets a GID the moment our own push
records it.** So the drift branch was adopting Jobber's `startAt` onto Calendar- and
generator-mastered rows, which is exactly the write **Visit Calendar rule #7 forbids**. It suppresses
the push while doing it, so our copy moved to match Jobber and the Calendar never re-asserted.
Silent in both directions.

| row's `source` | schedule rewrites it had made | owned by that script? |
|---|---|---|
| `supabase_cron` | 24 | no |
| `visit-calendar` | 9 | no |
| `jobber` | 1 | yes |

**33 of 34 landed on rows it does not own.** Structural, not bad luck: only **2** scheduled
`source='jobber'` visits exist in the whole database, against 710 `supabase_cron`.

Fixed by gating the drift branch on `visits.source='jobber'`, which is what ADR-015 already
specified. Refused drift is counted, printed, and written to the run JSON as
`drift_skipped_not_ours`, so suppressing the write does not also hide the disagreement.

🛑 **The gate is on the branch, not the candidate query** — the orphan branch must keep seeing every
source, or a visit deleted in Jobber would sit on the Calendar forever with a dead GID.
