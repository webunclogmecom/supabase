**Status: OPEN, needs Fred's decision. Nothing in the database has been changed.**
Found 2026-08-24 while cross-validating `rpa-derm-monthly` against the database.

**The data question is settled.** The link is wrong: 175-PV's grease was not on the 2026-07-22 load.
**The remaining question is regulatory, not technical**, and that is why nothing was written — see
[What actually needs deciding](#what-actually-needs-deciding).

## The contradiction in one line

Visit **6756** (175-PV Pura Vida Brickell 701) is dated **2026-07-29** and is linked to white manifest
**830673**, dumped **2026-07-22**. The pickup is dated **seven days after its own offload**. Grease is
pumped before it is dumped, so one of the two records is wrong.

It reaches the Miami-Dade **Liquid Waste Transporter** monthly filing through
`derm.v_lwt_monthly_rows` → `rpa-derm-monthly`, where it reads as waste offloaded before it was
collected.

## Why the link is wrong, not the date

1. **Photographic proof the trap was not pumped.** Two photos from the 07-19 attempt, stamped
   `Jul 19, 2026 at 10:09:03 PM` and `10:22:55 PM`, both at `701 Brickell Ave, Miami FL 33131`. The
   second shows the interceptor open with a solid grey grease cap still in place. Nothing was
   collected.
   > ⚠ **`photos.exif_taken_at` is NULL on all 24 rows for this visit, and so are the GPS columns.**
   > Read the table alone and you conclude there is no datable photographic evidence. There is: the
   > crew's camera app **burns the timestamp and street address into the pixels**. Metadata stripped,
   > evidence intact. Look at the image before concluding the photos cannot be dated.
2. **The Jobber note.** *"Couldn't compete the job because the PTO had broken."* The power take-off
   drives the vacuum pump.
3. **The business itself reversed the completion**, because the job had not been done. Only 4 such
   reversals exist in the whole audit history.
4. **The job was re-run 07-29**, completed 07-30 06:02, with a second note and a separate batch of
   10 photos ten days after the first.
5. **The peer pattern is unanimous.** Every other DERM-required visit dated 2026-07-29 went onto a
   load dumped **later**: visit 6794 (224-MP) → 310429 dumped 07-30; visits 6039 (042-MT) and 6104
   (062-TCE) → 831938 dumped 08-04. Only 6756 points backwards.
6. **The other four visits on load 830673 are all clean:** 7089 (065-TCE) 07-13, 6145 (068-TCE),
   6257 (092-TCE) and 6018 (036-LG) all 07-20 — every one before the 07-22 dump.
7. **175-PV has exactly two visits between 2026-06-15 and 2026-09-01:** 5819 on 06-19 (unlinked) and
   6756 on 07-29. There is no other visit this link could correctly point at.

### 🛑 The apparent corroboration is circular, and this is the trap

Three things look like independent confirmation and are not. All three descend from the single
2026-07-22 click:

- **`derm_manifests.client_id = 343` proves nothing.** `trg_aa_link_same_client` *enforces*
  `manifest.client_id = visit.client_id`. It is a tautology, not a witness.
- **The Stamp Studio "paper card" was generated FROM the link.** `derm.address_row_map` id 864
  (manifest 1628) has `facility_name_read = NULL`, `address_read = NULL`, `source = 'derm-link'`,
  `flags = {"card_from_link": true}` — `fn_card_from_link` materialises it on link insert. All five
  cards for ticket 830673 are like this. **The paper for 830673 was never OCR'd.** By contrast
  175-PV's three earlier manifests (825167, 819643, 819503) all carry genuine `claude-vision-v1`
  reads: *"Pura Vida" / "701 Brickell Av"*.
- **`derm.v_lwt_monthly_rows` is a view over the same link.**

### And the paper itself is weaker than it looks

The scanned FOG eManifest (`manifests/derm/1625/address_1.JPG`) **does list** generator #2 as
`GDO-02560 · 175-PV Pura Vida Brickell 701 · 701 Brickell Avenue`. But that generator list is
**typed** — we print it from our own route plan before the run — while everything genuinely observed
on site is handwritten (decal C0976, plate R3K543, capacity 2000). **Every per-generator column is
blank**: FOG control device type, capacity, wet volume, all measurements, and the per-generator
gallons and date. Someone drew a large "FOG" across section C instead of filling it. Only the facility
total is written (2000 gal, exactly the truck's stated full capacity).

So the paper records **who was on the planned route**, not **whose trap was actually pumped**. It is
not independent of the same route plan that produced the visit.

## The hidden cost of leaving it

Because 6756 *is* linked, it does not appear in `manifest_pickable_visits`. **The real 07-29 pickup is
invisible to the "needs a manifest" workflow**, and client 343 has **no manifest dumped after
2026-07-22 at all**. The grease collected on 07-29 is unmanifested, and the bad link is what is
hiding that.

## What actually needs deciding

The DB fix is one statement. The reason it has not been run is that it is only half the problem.

```sql
-- back up to backups/ first
DELETE FROM public.manifest_visits WHERE manifest_id = 1628 AND visit_id = 6756;
```

Manifest row 1628 stays (175-PV is printed on the physical sheet; deleting our copy would erase the
record of what was filed) and visit 6756 stays with its date untouched. Precedent:
`2026-07-15_unlink_009cn_impossible_manifest_visit.sql`, and visit 7090 (112-YA), un-completed by the
same cron on 2026-07-30, now `skipped` and already unlinked by hand.

**Fred's calls, and they are not technical:**

1. **The paper filed with Miami-Dade lists a generator whose grease was not collected.** Does DERM
   need a correction, or is a pre-printed route sheet with blank per-generator columns understood not
   to assert per-generator pickup? Unlinking makes our database honest and leaves the county copy
   saying something else, unflagged.
2. **Was the 2026-07 LWT report already submitted?** If it went out containing this row, the
   submission and the database will now disagree.
3. **175-PV's real 07-29 pickup has no manifest.** Once the link is removed it becomes visible in the
   pickable-visits workflow, and Diego will need to file it or explain it.
4. Worth one question to **Mark Noltion**, who signed the dump ticket and is `completed_by` on 6756:
   was 701 Brickell pumped on the night of 07-21/22 by a repaired truck? Nothing in the data suggests
   it, but he is the only person who can say so outright.

**Either way, do not clamp the dates in `derm.v_lwt_monthly_rows` or `rpa-derm-monthly`.** Considered
and rejected 2026-08-24: hiding an anomaly on a regulator-facing report is worse than printing one.

## What WAS shipped on 2026-08-24, and what it does not cover

`docs/migrations/2026-08-24_1510_manifest_link_completed_visit_guard.sql`:

- **`trg_ad_link_visit_completed`** on `public.manifest_visits` — a link may not be made against a
  visit that has not happened. ⚠ **This would NOT have stopped this row**: visit 6756 *was* marked
  completed on 07-22. Defence in depth for the adjacent hole; costs nothing (0 of 690 current links).
- **`trg_ae_dump_date_keeps_links_valid`** on `public.derm_manifests` — the manifest side, and the
  `NULL → value` hole `trg_ac` deliberately allows.
- **`derm.v_manifest_link_date_conflicts`** — the detector, and the **only** thing covering this case.
  Its `visit_not_completed` kind fires the moment a reconciler reverses a completion on a linked
  visit, which here was 13:46 on 07-29.
- **`rpa-derm-monthly` returns `data_quality.conflicts`** and a per-row `anomaly`, so this cannot be
  filed unknowingly. The row is still returned, never hidden. Read `data_quality.checked`: `false`
  means the overlay query failed and an empty list proves nothing.

**The visits side is deliberately not blocked.** The writer that broke this link was an automated
reconciler recording the truth that the job had not happened; blocking it would force the database to
keep asserting a service that never occurred. ⚠ And do not "just put the check in
`ripple_reschedule_visit`" — it refuses completed visits outright (line 19), and all 690 linked
visits are completed, so a check there can essentially never fire. See the migration header.
